Protection Against Child Marriage

Child marriage is a marriage in which one or both contracting parties are below the legal age of marriage. In India, the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 provides a legal framework for preventing child marriages and protecting children affected by them.

⚖️ Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006

The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 is the principal legislation dealing with child marriage in India. It replaced the earlier Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929.

👧 Who is a Child?

Under the Act, a female is a child if she has not completed 18 years of age, while a male is a child if he has not completed 21 years of age.

🚫 Marriage Before Legal Age

A marriage involving a person below the applicable legal age can be treated as a child marriage under the Act and can attract legal consequences.

🛡️ Protection of Children

The law provides mechanisms for preventing child marriages and for seeking protection and relief for children affected by such marriages.

⚖️ Punishment

Persons who perform, conduct, direct or promote a child marriage may face punishment under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006.

👮 Prevention of Child Marriage

The Act provides for Child Marriage Prohibition Officers and other authorities to help prevent child marriages and create awareness about the law.

📋 What Can Be Done if a Child Marriage is Planned?

  • Inform the appropriate local authorities or Child Marriage Prohibition Officer.
  • Seek help from the police or child-protection authorities when immediate intervention is required.
  • A child or concerned person can seek appropriate legal protection and assistance.
  • If a child is in immediate danger, contact emergency services.
💗 Remember: Child marriage can affect a child's education, health, safety, independence and future opportunities. Children should be protected from forced or underage marriage and should have access to appropriate support.

Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, commonly known as the POSH Act, provides protection to women against sexual harassment at the workplace and establishes mechanisms for prevention and redressal of complaints.

📋 About the Act

The Act is an important law for creating a safe and respectful working environment for women. It provides a legal framework for preventing sexual harassment and for handling complaints through designated complaint mechanisms.

Act No. 14 of 2013
The Act applies throughout India and came into force on 9 December 2013.

🚫 What is Sexual Harassment?

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome acts or behaviour of a sexual nature at the workplace. The law provides protection against such conduct and establishes a mechanism for making and resolving complaints.

Sexual harassment may include conduct such as unwanted physical contact or advances, demands or requests for sexual favours, sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography, or other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

🏢 What is a Workplace?

The meaning of workplace under the Act is broad. It can include offices and organisations in the public and private sectors, educational institutions, hospitals, nursing homes, sports institutions and other places connected with work.

A workplace can also include places visited by an employee during the course of employment, including transportation provided in connection with employment.

💼 Safe Workplace

Women have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment and to be treated with dignity and respect.

📝 Making a Complaint

A woman facing sexual harassment can use the complaint mechanism established under the Act and approach the appropriate complaints committee.

🏢 Internal Committee

Employers are required to establish an Internal Committee at workplaces covered by the Act, including offices or branches having 10 or more employees.

🏛️ Local Committee

The Act provides for a Local Committee at the district level to receive complaints in situations covered by the law, including certain cases where an Internal Committee is not available.

⚖️ Complaint and Inquiry Process

The complaints mechanism under the Act provides a procedure for dealing with allegations of sexual harassment. The committee conducts an inquiry according to the procedure prescribed by the Act and Rules.

1
Complaint

A complaint can be made through the appropriate complaints committee.

2
Conciliation

Conciliation may be considered where the complainant requests it, subject to the Act.

3
Inquiry

Where required, the committee conducts an inquiry according to the prescribed procedure.

4
Report and Action

After the inquiry, the report is submitted to the appropriate authority for further action as required by law.

⏱️ Important Time Limits

90 Days

The inquiry is required to be completed within the time period prescribed by the Act.

60 Days

After completion of the inquiry, the appropriate authority is required to take action on the recommendations within the prescribed period.

🔒 Confidentiality

The Act provides confidentiality protections concerning complaints and inquiry proceedings. Information relating to the complaint and inquiry should not be disclosed except as permitted by law.

📚 Employer Responsibilities

  • Provide a workplace environment that is safe and free from sexual harassment.
  • Constitute the required Internal Committee where applicable.
  • Display information relating to the Act and consequences of sexual harassment at the workplace.
  • Conduct awareness and sensitisation programmes as required.
  • Provide the required support for handling complaints under the Act.
  • Comply with the obligations prescribed under the Act and Rules.

🛡️ Protection for Women

The Act is intended to protect women against sexual harassment in workplaces across different sectors. The definition of an aggrieved woman is broad and can cover women irrespective of their employment status in circumstances covered by the Act.

⚠️ False or Malicious Complaints

The Act contains provisions dealing with complaints that are proved to have been made maliciously or with knowledge that the allegations were false, subject to the requirements of the law.

Important: A complaint should not be treated as false merely because it could not be proved. The legal requirements for action relating to malicious or false complaints must be followed.

🌸 Why Workplace Awareness Matters

Awareness of workplace sexual-harassment protections helps women understand their rights and helps organisations create safer and more respectful workplaces.

Employees should know who the appropriate complaints committee members are and how to report concerns within their organisation.

💗 Remember: Every woman has the right to a workplace where she can work with dignity and safety. If you experience sexual harassment, you can seek help through the appropriate complaints mechanism and, where appropriate, other legal or support services.

Women's Property & Inheritance Rights

Women's property rights are property and inheritance rights enjoyed by women as a category within a society. These rights can relate to ownership, inheritance, access to land, use of property and control over resources.

📖 What Are Property Rights?

Property rights are claims to property that are legally and socially recognised and enforceable by an authorised authority.

Land rights can include different legitimate claims to land and the benefits or products produced from that land.

These rights may include ownership or rights to use property.

🌱 Land, Property and Inheritance

Property and land rights can arise through inheritance, transfers from the State, tenancy arrangements and purchase of land.

Having access to property and land can have important economic and social consequences for women and their families.

⭐ Important Areas

🏡 Ownership

Property ownership can provide women with greater control over resources and can strengthen their economic independence.

📜 Inheritance

Inheritance is one of the ways through which property and land can be transferred to family members.

🌾 Land Rights

Access to land can be important for women, particularly in rural and agricultural communities where land may support livelihoods and food production.

💰 Economic Independence

Greater access to property and resources can support women's economic security, decision-making and independence.

⚖️ Impact of Gender Bias

Traditional systems of property ownership and inheritance have often resulted in men receiving greater control over property and resources.

Gender-biased practices can reduce women's ability to control property, make financial decisions and improve their economic position.

👨‍👩‍👧 Patriarchal Property Systems

In some societies, property and inheritance practices have traditionally followed male family lines.

Such practices can make women dependent on male relatives and can limit their influence over how property is used or transferred.

💍 Marriage and Property Rights

Women's access to property can sometimes be affected by marriage, divorce or the death of a spouse.

Traditional marriage practices may also influence how families understand women's rights to property and inheritance.

🚧 Barriers to Property Rights

⚠️ Inadequate Laws and Enforcement

Even where laws provide property or inheritance rights, weak implementation and enforcement can create difficulties in exercising those rights.

📚 Lack of Awareness

Limited awareness of existing laws and legal remedies can make it difficult for women to understand or claim their rights.

👥 Traditional Attitudes

Traditional gender expectations and stereotypes can act as barriers to women's ownership and control of property.

🏛️ Administrative Barriers

Property and inheritance claims may involve administrative systems where lack of legal awareness or unequal decision-making can create additional difficulties.

📢 Why Awareness Matters

Awareness of property and inheritance rights can help women understand the importance of ownership, land access and control over resources.

Understanding available legal rights and legal redress options can also help women seek appropriate assistance when their rights are denied.

🌸 Social and Economic Importance

Greater access to property can have economic, psychological and social benefits for women.

Property ownership and secure access to land can contribute to greater economic security, decision-making power and improved opportunities for women and their families.

💡 Remember: Property and inheritance rights can play an important role in women's economic independence and security. Women should be aware of the property rights available to them under the applicable law.

For an India-specific property or inheritance dispute, the applicable law and current legal provisions should be checked and appropriate legal advice should be obtained.

Protection from Cyber Abuse

Cyber crimes are offences committed using computers, mobile devices, networks or the internet. Online abuse can affect women even when the perpetrator does not have physical contact with the victim or does not reveal their identity.

🚨 Common Forms of Cyber Abuse

👀 Cyber Stalking

Repeatedly contacting or monitoring a woman through the internet, email or other electronic communication can amount to stalking when the legal requirements are satisfied.

💬 Cyber Bullying

Online bullying can include repeated abusive, threatening or humiliating behaviour through digital platforms.

📱 Cyber Harassment

Unwanted online communication, threats, abusive messages or sexually inappropriate content may form part of cyber harassment.

🪪 Identity Theft

Using another person's password, electronic signature or other unique identification feature dishonestly or fraudulently is addressed by Section 66C of the Information Technology Act.

🔐 Privacy Violation

Section 66E of the Information Technology Act addresses capturing, publishing or transmitting images of a private area of a person without consent in circumstances violating privacy.

📸 Voyeurism

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 contains provisions concerning voyeurism, including watching, capturing or disseminating images of a woman engaged in a private act in circumstances covered by the law.

⚖️ Current Criminal-Law Provisions

The older source material refers to Sections 354A, 354C, 354D, 499, 503, 507 and 509 of the Indian Penal Code. These IPC provisions should not be presented as the current criminal-law provisions because the IPC was replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 from 1 July 2024.

⚠️ BNS Section 75

Section 75 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 deals with sexual harassment, including specified unwelcome sexual conduct, demands for sexual favours, showing pornography against a woman's will and sexually coloured remarks.

👀 BNS Section 78

Section 78 deals with stalking and includes monitoring a woman's use of the internet, email or other electronic communication.

📸 BNS Section 77

Section 77 deals with voyeurism, including watching, capturing or disseminating images of a woman engaging in a private act in circumstances covered by the provision.

🚨 BNS Section 351

Section 351 deals with criminal intimidation, including threats involving a person's body, reputation or property. Anonymous threats are also addressed by the provision.

📢 BNS Section 79

Section 79 addresses words, sounds, gestures, objects or conduct intended to insult the modesty of a woman, including intrusion upon her privacy.

📝 BNS Section 356

Section 356 deals with defamation. Whether an online publication constitutes an offence depends on the facts and applicable law.

💻 Information Technology Act, 2000

The Information Technology Act, 2000 contains provisions dealing with several forms of computer-related and electronic offences.

🪪 Section 66C

Deals with punishment for identity theft, including dishonest or fraudulent use of another person's electronic signature, password or unique identification feature.

🔒 Section 66E

Deals with violation of privacy through capturing, publishing or transmitting images of a private area without consent in circumstances covered by the Act.

🚫 Section 67

Deals with publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form.

🔞 Section 67A

Deals with publishing or transmitting material containing sexually explicit acts in electronic form.

🧒 Section 67B

Deals with electronic material depicting children in sexually explicit acts or conduct covered by the provision.

📸 Non-Consensual Sharing of Intimate Content

Sharing or transmitting intimate or sexually explicit material without consent can have serious legal consequences. Depending on the facts, provisions of the Information Technology Act and criminal law may apply.

A victim should preserve relevant evidence and seek appropriate legal assistance rather than continuing to engage with or threaten the perpetrator.

🛡️ What to Do if You Face Cyber Abuse

📸 Preserve Evidence

Keep screenshots, messages, emails, profile links, URLs, dates and other relevant digital evidence.

🔐 Secure Your Accounts

Change compromised passwords, enable two-factor authentication and review account security settings.

🚫 Report the Content

Use the reporting and blocking tools provided by the relevant social-media or online platform where appropriate.

👮 Seek Legal Help

Depending on the circumstances, a victim can approach the police, cyber-crime authorities, legal-aid services or another appropriate authority.

💗 Remember: Online abuse is serious. Save evidence, protect your accounts and seek appropriate help when you face stalking, threats, harassment, identity theft, privacy violations or non-consensual intimate content.

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (Act No. 43 of 2005) provides legal protection and remedies for women who face domestic violence. The Act was enacted to provide more effective protection of the rights of women who are victims of violence occurring within the family. The Act came into force on 26 October 2006.

📖 What is Domestic Violence?

Section 3 of the Act defines domestic violence broadly. It includes physical, sexual, verbal and emotional abuse and economic abuse. It also covers certain unlawful demands for dowry, property or valuable security, threats, and conduct causing physical or mental harm.

🏠 Right to Reside in the Shared Household

Section 17 recognises an aggrieved woman's right to reside in the shared household. This right is not simply dependent on whether the house is owned or registered in her name.

⭐ Important Features of the Act

The Act provides several forms of protection and relief to women facing domestic violence.

🏠 Protection and Residence

  • Right to reside in the shared household under Section 17.
  • Protection against domestic violence.
  • Residence orders may be granted by the court.
  • Protection can cover different forms of domestic abuse.

💰 Economic Protection

  • Economic abuse is recognised under the Act.
  • Monetary relief may be available under Section 20.
  • Relief may include expenses and maintenance for the woman and her children, where ordered.

💗 Emotional and Verbal Abuse

  • Verbal and emotional abuse is covered.
  • Psychological harm may form part of domestic violence.
  • Threats and conduct causing mental harm may also be relevant.

👧 Child Custody

  • Temporary custody orders may be granted under Section 21.
  • The court may make appropriate arrangements regarding children.

⚖️ Main Remedies Available

Depending on the circumstances of the case, an aggrieved woman may seek different types of relief under the Act.

🛡️ Section 18 – Protection Order

A court may pass a protection order to protect the aggrieved person from acts of domestic violence.

🏠 Section 19 – Residence Order

The Magistrate may pass appropriate residence orders in accordance with the Act.

💰 Section 20 – Monetary Relief

Monetary relief may be ordered for expenses, losses and maintenance as provided under the Act.

👧 Section 21 – Custody Order

Temporary custody of children may be granted where appropriate.

💗 Section 22 – Compensation

Compensation may be awarded for injuries, including mental torture and emotional distress, as provided under the Act.

⏳ Section 23 – Interim Orders

The Magistrate may grant interim and, where legally appropriate, ex parte orders.

👩‍⚖️ Protection Officers

Protection Officers are appointed by the Government under the Act. Their duties include assisting an aggrieved woman, facilitating access to legal aid and relief, assisting with Domestic Incident Reports, and helping coordinate the implementation of orders with the appropriate authorities.

🤝 Service Providers

Notified service providers, including eligible organisations, may assist women with Domestic Incident Reports and may help provide or arrange services such as shelter, counselling and medical assistance as provided under the Act.

📍 Where Can a Woman Seek Help?

An aggrieved woman may approach the appropriate Magistrate's court, Protection Officer, police station, service provider or other available support channel depending on her circumstances.

⏱️ 60-Day Provision

Section 12(5) provides that the Magistrate shall endeavour to dispose of every application made under the Act within 60 days from the date of its first hearing.

⚖️ Legal Aid and Other Proceedings

Women may be eligible for free legal services according to applicable legal-aid provisions. Relief under the Domestic Violence Act can also operate alongside other legal proceedings where applicable.

💡 Remember: A woman facing domestic violence does not have to remain silent. The law provides different forms of protection and relief, depending on the facts of the situation.

Dowry System in India

Dowry refers to property, valuable goods, money or other articles that may be demanded or transferred in connection with a marriage. When such property or valuable security is demanded as a condition or consideration for marriage, the practice can become a serious social and legal issue. The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 is the principal legislation dealing with dowry in India.

📖 What is Dowry?

Dowry generally refers to property or valuable security given or agreed to be given, directly or indirectly, by one party to a marriage to the other party, or by the parents or other persons to either party, in connection with the marriage, as covered by the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.

⚖️ Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961

The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 was enacted to prohibit the giving and taking of dowry and to address demands for dowry connected with marriage. The Act is an important part of India's legal framework for preventing dowry-related practices.

⭐ Important Points

🚫 Giving and Taking Dowry

  • The law prohibits giving or taking dowry.
  • Dowry-related transactions can have legal consequences.
  • The law applies to dowry connected with marriage.

⚠️ Dowry Demands

  • Demanding dowry in connection with marriage is prohibited.
  • Pressure or coercion to obtain property or valuable security may be associated with dowry-related offences.
  • Women and their families can seek appropriate legal assistance.

💗 Dowry Harassment

  • Dowry-related demands may be accompanied by harassment or cruelty.
  • Such conduct may also involve domestic violence.
  • Depending on the circumstances, additional legal provisions may apply.

🛡️ Protection of Women

  • Women facing dowry-related harassment can seek help through appropriate legal channels.
  • Protection and other relief may be available under applicable laws.
  • Legal aid may be available to eligible persons.

🚨 Dowry-Related Crimes

Dowry-related abuse can take different forms. It may involve harassment, cruelty, physical or emotional abuse, domestic violence, threats, coercion, or in serious cases, death. The legal consequences depend on the facts and the applicable law.

💔 Cruelty and Harassment

Repeated harassment or cruelty connected with unlawful demands for property or valuable security can have serious legal consequences.

🏠 Domestic Violence

Dowry-related abuse may also fall within the forms of domestic violence recognised under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005.

⚠️ Serious Violence

In serious cases, dowry-related harassment can be associated with physical violence, suicide, or death. Such cases require immediate legal intervention and protection.

📢 Report and Seek Help

A woman facing dowry-related threats or violence should consider contacting the police, legal-aid services, Protection Officers, or other appropriate support services.

🌸 Social Impact of Dowry

The dowry system can create financial pressure on the bride's family and can contribute to inequality and dependence within marriage. Dowry-related demands may also lead to emotional abuse, harassment and violence against women.

📢 Why Awareness Matters

Awareness about dowry laws can help women and families understand their rights and recognise unlawful demands. Marriage should not become a reason for demanding money, property or valuable security from a woman or her family.

💡 Remember: Dowry is not a condition that a woman or her family should have to accept for marriage. If someone is facing a dowry-related demand, harassment or violence, appropriate legal and support services can be approached.

Police & Complaint Support

Reporting an offence to the police can be an important step towards protection, investigation, justice and access to support services. Women reporting crimes should be treated with dignity and their safety and privacy should be respected.

🚨 1. Immediate Safety

If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, move to a safe place where possible and contact emergency services. In India, emergency assistance can be reached through 112.

📝 2. Registration of an FIR

For information relating to a cognisable offence, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 provides the framework for recording information by the police.

📄 FIR Copy

Ask for the FIR number and a copy of the recorded information for your records.

👮 Investigating Officer

Keep the name and designation of the investigating officer handling the case.

📍 3. Zero FIR

Zero FIR refers to the practice of registering information concerning a cognisable offence at a police station even when the offence falls outside that police station's territorial jurisdiction, followed by transfer to the appropriate police station.

👩‍✈️ 4. Statement of a Woman

In appropriate cases, particularly involving sexual offences, statements may be recorded by a woman police officer or woman officer in accordance with applicable law and procedure.

A survivor can communicate concerns about privacy, safety, language or the need for appropriate support.

🏥 5. Medical Examination

Survivors of sexual violence should receive prompt medical attention. Medical and forensic examination should follow the applicable legal procedure and informed consent requirements.

In an emergency, seek medical treatment immediately rather than waiting for police formalities.

📸 6. Preserve Evidence

📱 Digital Evidence

  • Messages
  • Emails
  • Screenshots
  • Social-media profiles
  • Photos and videos

📋 Other Evidence

  • Medical reports
  • Photographs of injuries
  • Relevant documents
  • CCTV information
  • Witness details

⚠️ 7. If Police Refuse to Register a Complaint

If information disclosing a cognisable offence is not appropriately recorded, a person can seek the remedies available under the applicable criminal procedure law, including approaching senior police authorities and other appropriate legal authorities.

Legal Services Authorities can also provide assistance to eligible persons who require legal aid.

🔎 8. During Investigation

Police investigation may include recording statements, examining the scene, collecting evidence, identifying witnesses, taking lawful action against accused persons and submitting the appropriate report before the court.

💗 9. Support After Reporting

⚖️ Legal Aid

Eligible survivors can seek assistance through legal services authorities.

🧠 Counselling

Psychological and emotional support can be important during recovery.

🏠 Shelter Support

Appropriate shelter and protection services may be available depending on the circumstances.

🛡️ Protection

Where necessary, survivors can seek appropriate protection and safety measures.

💗 Remember: Reporting a crime is an important step, but a survivor does not have to face the process alone. Preserve relevant evidence, seek appropriate support and prioritise personal safety.

Financial & Family Support

Women may have legal rights and protections relating to family property, inheritance, maintenance, maternity and financial relief. These rights depend on the applicable law and the circumstances of the individual case.

🏠 1. Property & Inheritance Rights

Women's property and inheritance rights in India can depend on the applicable personal law, religion and other circumstances.

📜 Hindu Succession Act, 1956

The Hindu Succession Act provides rules relating to inheritance and succession for persons covered by Hindu personal law.

👩‍👧‍👦 Daughters' Rights

The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 gave daughters equal coparcenary status with sons in the Hindu joint family system.

💵 2. Maintenance & Family Financial Support

The source material discusses maintenance rights available to wives, children and parents under applicable family and maintenance laws.

👨‍👩‍👧 Family Maintenance

  • Eligible family members may seek financial maintenance under applicable law.
  • Maintenance can help meet necessary living expenses.
  • The exact right and procedure depend on the applicable law and circumstances.

🛡️ 3. Financial Relief in Domestic Violence

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 provides remedies and protections for women facing domestic violence.

💰 Monetary & Economic Relief

The Act includes remedies relating to monetary relief and economic abuse in appropriate cases.

🤰 4. Maternity & Financial Protection

Article 42 of the Constitution directs the State to make provisions for securing just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.

📋 Maternity Benefit Act, 1961

The source identifies the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 as legislation relating to maternity protection for women.
